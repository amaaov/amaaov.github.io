Holding Topology

Boris Fontanov · 19 August 2026 · Helsinki

I've been looking for a strict definition of juggling and for its difference from object manipulation. The turn was seeing that, at a single moment, the state is two at once: something held and something already unheld. Hold and unhold are quantities measured here, and from them come four states that pass into one another.

The system I look at is body, objects, and environment. Body here is what retains: a palm, a pinch, a press against the chest, a ball parked on a foot, several people who share the same objects, a robot. An object is the stillest member, almost never changing itself, held or unheld. Environment is the noisiest and is outside the body's command: gravity and air in flight, floor and wall in bounce, a strike that only collides, a draught, an uneven ball. Kratos and akrateia are read on the objects the body holds or that are already unheld. Environment acts more strongly on what is already unheld and increases the uncertainty of each trajectory, the entropy of the system.

Total unhold is : every object in is unheld at once. Shannon treated staying in continuously as a mathematical limit of zero dwell. Robotics studies impact systems on that limit, where force arrives in pulses (Sanfelice, Teel, and Sepulchre, 2007; Ruggiero, Lippiello, and Siciliano, 2018; Ramadani et al., 2026). Bounce, a colliding kick, and a paddle strike in human hands still return the object to holding: the system enters and crosses again.

Object manipulation lives in kratos. Juggling keeps akrateia and kratos together and may briefly enter total akrateia.
