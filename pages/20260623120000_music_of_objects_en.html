Music of Objects

Scenes, Synchronization, Error, and Finding Your Own Relations

amaaåv · 23 June 2026 · Helsinki

The tone stalls before the sound arrives. I hold the poi at the top of the arc, the room already listening, and the cheap speaker on the shelf still has nothing to say. Three slow circles, one stall, then I wait for a tone that was supposed to come half a second late. It comes with the stall instead, exactly where I wanted motion and tone to stay apart. My shoulders tighten against empty time as the pause I meant to keep collapses, and that collapse is the first real note of the evening, bound to this room on this evening; another life would need its own pass.

I

Situation, experiment, experience

A scene, an attempt, and an experiment each depend on the exact situation I am in: this shoulder, this cable, this friendship, this hunger, this weather, whether anyone is watching, how I slept. The vocabulary below is for one pass through a lived moment, where the situation itself sets what can be tried and anything remains possible. What I write down is experiment; what arrives in the body and attention is experience, and that experience is inevitable whether or not I hold an object, whether or not a speaker is switched on.

Authority arrives as tutorial, tradition, admired performer, or inner critic, and none of them know this shoulder tonight. The voice that treats the pass as something to justify can wait as listening and trying show what returns.

Music can begin with an object moving through space and a relation that fails on schedule. It can also begin with neither object nor sound: sitting, breathing, listening to a radiator, watching whether thought reaches for a pattern. A poi, a ball, a staff, a hand, a rope, a cursor, a line of code, a breath, a spoken instruction, a pause, a failure to move, a sound that arrives too late, or no sound at all may be material when the situation offers them. The usual training path runs through repertoire, technique, known works, tradition, and that path teaches hands, while another path teaches relations between movement, sound, attention, intention, and error inside the situation you are actually inside.

The core unit is the scene: a small experimental frame tied to one situation (what is here, what is attempted, what comes back, what differs), smaller than a composition and tighter than free improvisation. The working line runs intention → action → difference → error → awareness → repetition → variation → scene, and the aim is to make error visible, audible, repeatable, and useful in the pass you are living.

Constraints, when I use them, name amounts of freedom: what I have defined for this pass, what I already know, what I have already tried, what the body and material physically allow. They are a deliberate narrowing so something becomes observable. They expire when the situation changes. Physical limitation is real, and moral limitation dressed as technique remains optional.

Being-here comes first, and experience arrives whether the pass succeeds or fails. I can sit with no object and no sound source and still be inside an experiment, because attention is already moving; the experiment is what I choose to do with that movement, and the experience comes anyway.

II

Sound without a source

Tutorials often propose mapping poi height to pitch. For the first minutes such a map listens clearly, then turns into a leash, and the object already serves the synth. You can keep the speaker, drop that obvious map, and try brightness without body, width without melody, dryness when the circle comes close to the chest. The scene grows harder to describe and easier to feel. A tutorial rule holds in some rooms; another evening asks for a different map.

Sound, in this framework, is wider than vibration. Silence counts, remembered sound counts, and expected sound that never arrives counts. A scene may have no sound-producing object at all; it may have no object at all. Movement, if there is movement, gathers attention around no-sound or around the possibility of sound; stillness does the same. You can sit without poi, switch the speaker off, and stay with that emptiness for three minutes without inventing a chore to escape it: restlessness comes first, then a slower kind of listening. Movement can arrange how you listen without commanding sound, and listening may be the whole scene.

III

Balance as catching

Balance in object work resembles continuous falling and catching: a poi pattern is recovered each beat; voice rides breath, pressure, pitch, intention; a script is a trace of decisions under correction; improvisation navigates local instabilities. Falling, catching, redistribution, and attention meet in that balance, and in a good scene the person chooses what to do with the instability.

The same shows up in juggling and again in a night script when the old version already lies: weight leaves stability, shifts, loses phase, overcorrects, finds a short interval of good distribution, and falls again. The living structure lives in the fall and the catch; the pose between them is brief.

IV

What a scene holds

A scene begins with the situation in front of you: what is actually here, what this body can do today, what material is in the room, and what relation you want to test in this pass. The questions remain prompts for one experiment, an open door for this pass, and the scene itself makes the material of this situation observable.

One carded pass can read as “Delayed stall”: evening practice, one working speaker, left shoulder tired, object poi, sound condition a short tone or silence if no source is active, duration five minutes. Intention is to make a circle, stop the poi, and let the response arrive late; action runs circle → stall → hold, and this pass allows only slow circles and stalls, without fast tricks, decoration, or changing the pattern too early. Synchronization mode is delay, expected difference is leaving the pause too soon, variation lets the response appear only after every third stall, recording is video and one sentence of notes, and completion is to find three pauses where delay changes the feeling of the scene.

Another pass, another situation: “Chair, no source”, morning, cold light, no props, no object, no active source, room sound only, three minutes. Intention is to stay with bare presence without adding a chore; action is to sit, breathe, and notice attention without getting up to fetch equipment. Expected difference is inventing a chore to escape bare presence; completion is to name one shift in attention without calling it achievement.

V

Relations between movement and response

Synchronization here is any structured relation between object movement, rhythm, sound, silence, intention, and attention, chosen for the situation at hand. Matching the beat is one corner of a much larger field. Every relation named below is a possible experiment you may take or leave for this pass.

Time relations first. Phase places sound in a specific part of the cycle, as when a tone appears only at the top point. Synchrony ties movement and sound to the same moment; shift delays or anticipates; upbeat prepares the accent and downbeat lands on it; delay lets sound follow movement like a trace; following makes sound chase the object; prediction announces the gesture before it happens; echo returns a reduced version of the previous action; canon repeats a line after a delay; loop cycles a fragment.

Metric relations layer counting structures, where polyrhythm shares one time field between different subdivisions, polymeter runs different cycle lengths beside each other, cross-accent avoids the expected pulse, metric failure breaks the count on purpose, and return to meter brings a drifting system back into alignment. With poi or juggling the body becomes a small ensemble: one hand in three, the other in four, steps in five, breath in another layer entirely.

5

V cont.

Space, spectrum, pause, behavior

Spatial relations map position and trajectory to sound or attention. Height, direction, radius, amplitude, approach, distance, symmetry, asymmetry, rotation — each can change density, panning, reverb, modulation, dryness, tension. Literal maps get tired fast, and indirect ones last longer: higher object as more brightness and less body; wider radius as wider stereo field; asymmetry as unstable noise; rotation as modulation.

Dynamic and spectral relations tie energy to timbre: speed to density, acceleration to attack, pressure to saturation, tension to narrowing, release to decay, tremor to grain, explosion to noise burst. Pause and silence deserve their own category, because silence can be total absence, freeze with active attention, loop of earlier material, framed emptiness, delayed expectation, reset, or held mass in the body while sound stays flat, and pause shows where attention, mass, and expectation sit.

Behavioral relations give the sound system character toward movement (mirror, imitation, following, resistance, ignoring, provocation, dialogue, conflict, autonomy), and style often lives in that character as obedient, late, stubborn, fragile, forgetful, too early, generous, dry, noisy, silent, or unstable.

Intention is another synchronization layer. You can declare the plan, hold a partial direction, release into observation, move freely, fall into automatism, let chance decide, take a foreign command, obey anti-instruction, prepare a precursor before the command arrives, or fail intention on purpose. Foreign intention, anti-instruction, and precursor link object work to theater, voice, and text on the machine more directly than any effect chain.

VI

The practice cycle

The simplest cycle: say the intention for this situation; perform the action; name the difference; repeat; increase speed; read from a score or follow another voice; do anything except the instruction; record the useful error.

Before acting, say what you are about to do in one sentence. “Three circles, one stall, then silence.” “The sound arrives after the movement.” “The fall sounds; the catch is silent.” “I will write the loop without a conditional.” Intention works better as a statement than as a question to an empty room, because it gives material you can build on at once, and if it stays foggy when spoken, the pass is probably too vague.

Keep the action short: ten seconds of clear motion beats five minutes of fog. Enter the pass already carrying a gesture or a clear bodily tone, so the room does not hang waiting for a plan, and name the gap while leaving “bad” and “wrong” for another room. “I moved before the sound.” “I left the pause too early.” “The left hand copied the right.” “I prepared the commanded move before hearing it.” “I wrote a familiar solution where the pass asked for a constrained one.” Unnamed error stays fog, and named error becomes material. When the difference is named, ask one more thing: if this is so, what else follows from it? Play one consequence in the next pass before returning to a fix.

If the same error repeats, habit is speaking, and speed exposes structure: try fifty, seventy, ninety, one hundred ten percent, then return to sixty. The return is often where hearing comes back after the edge of collapse.

External structure tests listening. A score can be as small as circle, pause, voice, silence, fall, catch, noise, repeat, error, stop; read it silently, speak it, or let someone else speak it at random.

Anti-instruction is stricter than opposition. The command “circle” asks for stall, silence, another plane (anything outside circle, half-circle, preparation, or disguised circle). “Louder” asks you to shift density, distance, grain, delay, dryness, silence before reaching for volume alone. In text, “use if” asks for another shape (tables, mapping, pattern matching, composition) without if, switch, ternary, or disguised guard clauses. The point is a wider reaction field.

VII

Exercises as rooms

One gesture, one sound: poi, ball, staff, hand, or cursor; a short tone, click, noise burst, or no sound; three minutes in which one gesture creates one response without decoration. Pass through synchrony, half-second delay, half-second prediction, response only every third gesture, then no sound while attention alone changes, and ask which relation was clearest, which had character, where error appeared, whether error could be repeated.

Pause as action keeps circles and stalls only: movement sounds while pause stays silent; movement silent while pause sounds; movement noisy and pause clean; the reverse; both silent while attention shifts. Expected error is leaving the pause too early; completion is one pause stronger than the movement before it.

Fall and catch work with two materials, noise and tone: fall sounds, catch silent; the reverse; sound between fall and catch; sound only on soft catch; no sound, only expectation. Record where risk peaks, where the body tenses, whether sound makes the catch too obvious, whether silence after the catch outweighs the accent.

Phase between two lines (hands, poi, hand and voice) begins together, drifts, and returns through quarter shift, half shift, free drift, forced return. Expected error is one line starting to control the other; completion is to hear the relation between the lines.

8

VII cont.

Resistance and error as vocabulary

Resistance breaks the obvious logic that bigger movement must mean bigger sound, and moves first through mirror, then resistance, then ignoring every third gesture, then fatigue weakening response, then large gesture producing silence that resets the scene. Irritation tells you something, and sometimes it becomes technique.

Error as vocabulary begins with a familiar pattern where slips stay visible. Assign functions to phase loss, tremor, early catch, late catch, extra rotation, wrong angle, drop, forgotten pause, premature preparation: phase loss as swing, tremor as tremolo, early catch as prediction, late catch as delay, extra rotation as ornament, wrong angle as modulation, drop as reset, forgotten pause as syncopation, premature preparation as precursor. Repeat one chosen error three times on purpose, each time heightening it or rhyming it with a neighboring gesture.

VIII

Voice

Voice is a tool for intention, and I often discover what I am doing only when I try to say it. Say one sentence, perform, then say whether it matched, did not match, or what differed, keeping the constraint to one sentence only.

Voice can replace the metronome. “One — two — three — pause.” “Circle — circle — stop — silence.” Nonsense syllables if melody would sneak in. Movement follows voice, voice follows movement, voice accelerates alone, movement accelerates alone, voice commands silence; the expected error is that voice starts serving habitual movement and stops directing it.

Voice can sound physical quality without singing: circle as “ooo”, sharp break as “k”, tremor as rolled consonant, fall as exhale, catch as closed consonant, pause as held breath, slow expansion as open vowel, collapse as noisy release.

IX

Text as score

A script on the machine fits the same cycle as a body in the room. Before writing, say one sentence. “I will touch only this file.” “I will make it run without the library everyone recommends.” “I will express the loop through composition.” Afterward compare whether you did exactly this, changed extra things, discovered a hidden appetite to impress, or made the intention too wide. If the intention spills past one sentence, the attempt is too large.

Read a short passage aloud as actions — take input, check it, build something, return something — then reverse the order: action score first, then the text, then comparison. The expected difference is often that the text performs an action absent from the score.

Anti-instruction widens the field: someone says “use a class”, and you find another shape; someone says “add a flag”, and you find a third form that sidesteps the flag entirely.

When something breaks, pause before the fix and ask what you expected, what happened, where the difference is, what check would have caught this, and what habit allowed it. Write the check, then fix along break → observation → check → correction → habit named; a break becomes useful when it changes the next pass.

X

Theater, improvisation, friendship

Choreography lends space, weight, time, flow. One gesture moves through low, middle, high level; forward, back, side, diagonal; small, medium, large size; straight, curved, spiral, broken path; then assign sound without default habits. Weight in four qualities — heavy slow, heavy fast, light slow, light fast — can be reversed so heavy movement makes light sound, and flow separates visible continuity from audible continuity.

Theater lends action, obstacle, status, repetition, subtext. Action can be to hold silence while the obstacle is the object that keeps demanding sound; perform five times with a stronger obstacle each pass. Repetition between two people runs A gestures, B copies, A copies B’s copy, and you watch what disappears, intensifies, becomes shared error, becomes interpretation. Status may give command to movement over sound, to sound over movement, or hold equal status in conflict; subtext keeps the same external action with an internal intention to hide error, reveal error, resist, ask for help, delay time, prevent sound, prepare too early.

Improvisation rests on offer and acceptance. First take what has already happened in the room (a gesture, a sound, a break, another person’s phrase) as fact, then add the next layer: another scale, silence, a move into voice or into structure. Erasing what happened throws out the material of the situation; better to give the break a place and justify it with the next move. Stay with the last event in one line (one answer to what just occurred) and let repetition rhyme a theme and heighten it across neighboring gestures. Accept without literal copy by repeating rhythm in another body part, answering with silence, turning error into accent, changing scale. When someone errs, accept the error as the new line, so if poi drops, work with falling; if voice cracks, keep the crack as timbre; if a line of text fails, stay inside the failure before repairing; if a command is misheard, let the mishearing open a new scene.

A partner who catches failure without humiliation changes the scene library, and friendship here is practical as shared recovery when the pass goes wrong.

XI

Session and record

Fifteen minutes can run: choose intention, speak the scene, perform slowly, increase speed, repeat one error on purpose, write notes. Forty-five minutes can alternate object and sound, voice or score, anti-instruction, then notes. Ninety minutes can separate movement without sound, sound without movement, relations, voice, text or written score, partner play, recorded form, a note about what to try next time.

After each scene, record briefly the date, scene name, situation, intention, what was said, what was done, difference, error, useful error, what speed destroyed, what external instruction to try next, possible anti-instruction, what to repeat, what to hold back on the next pass. One carded example: scene “Voice commands poi”, tired evening, one speaker; intention “Three circles, stall, sound after pause”; done sound with stall; difference delay collapsed early; error fear of empty time; useful yes, pause too short; speed destroyed command order; anti-instruction on “stall” anything except stall or its preparation; repeat delays at 0.5, 1, 2 seconds; hold back sound at the moment of stopping on the next pass.

Reusable scene types include said/done, delayed response, prediction, silent source, no sonic orientation, voice commands body, body commands voice, score reading, foreign command, anti-command, error as line, break as check, friend catches failure.

13

XI

Evaluating a scene

Ask whether intention was explicit, whether difference could be named, whether error appeared and could repeat, what changed at higher speed, whether the sound relation had character, whether silence functioned as material, whether the scene worked without an active sound source, whether another person could enter, whether the same scene could move between object, voice, and text. Half of those true is enough to keep the scene.

14

XII

Elsewhere

The same habits appear outside the practice room: a teacher insists there is one correct grip; a friend copies your mistake and makes it theirs; nobody answers and the silence carries weight; a habit returns every week until you name it; someone speaks louder and the room closes; people mimic each other without asking. The body starts before the counted moment because it already decided.

The object may be attention in conversation, the text under change in a local script, trust in friendship, or poi, sound, silence, and even absence in music. Object and sound slots can stay empty while the experiment remains, because situation and experience begin with being here before any permission arrives.

Build scenes where errors can appear, be named, repeated, transformed, and shared inside the situation you are actually living. A living pass falls, notices, catches, remembers, and changes form, and that is enough for one evening and for many evenings after, each different from the last.
