As I Change

Generative AI, competition, and an experience in which people change one another.

I imagine a working day that has finally become shorter. Some of the work has been done with generative AI, the result has been checked, and everything necessary is finished. There is still time before evening. What I like about the promise of such a day is this interval, whose contents are still undecided. I could finish early, linger over something interesting, spend time with another person. The choice is still open.

Then I begin to fill the space that has opened. Another attempt might turn out better than the first. It is cheaper now to make it, check it, set it alongside the other. I could try a different solution or return to a question put aside. Each continuation seems reasonable on its own, and the imagined day gradually grows more crowded than the one I meant to shorten.

Somewhere among these continuations, the meaning of finished work changes. A successful result already reveals the possibility of another, and stopping requires a separate decision. I imagine in advance what else I could get done. Later, someone may come to expect the same of me, but for now I am the first to hold myself to this new measure. Free time acquires a purpose before anyone asks me to account for it.

Live at this pace long enough, and you might begin preparing for it deliberately. Look for a shorter route, train your attention, collect ways of working that succeed, change them, compare the result with other people's work. I imagine people drawn to the competition itself. They want to become better at it, and they arrive at each new task carrying the experience of their earlier preparation. Others may join them, ready to train together.

In this future, the hardest thing for me to imagine is my own desire. I can imagine the work being good and the people attentive to one another. They may understand the task, notice mistakes, spare a teammate's energy. The better they do, the less my familiar explanations help me account for my reluctance. I am left with the simple feeling that I do not want to live for the next comparison.

But someone else's measure has already begun to act. In my mind I look at my work from the position of a person who could get it sooner, and I notice everything that slows the approach to a result already decided. Curiosity becomes a delay. Trying to understand something with another person begins to look expensive. I am still doing the same work, while my relationship to it has already changed.

It is hard to admit how readily I carry on this assessment myself. Nobody has to stand beside me and hurry me. Imagining someone better prepared is enough to make me start defending my pace before them. Under this imagined scrutiny, I gradually become part of the work that ought to be organized better. Even the wish to preserve something dear starts looking for an argument about its future usefulness.

I want to be able to finish what I begin and answer for the result. I need work to sustain everyday life, and new tools may help with it. That makes this difficulty hard to walk away from. It remains inside the choice of what to entrust to the machine, what to attend to, and which continuation to accept as my own. I can welcome greater speed while I can still recognize a chance to choose in the time it gives me.

With another person, I want to experience something for which I have no ready purpose. To hear them in a way that could change my own intention. To make room for a feeling that at first unsettles a familiar explanation. Sometimes this wish holds more vulnerability than agreeing to carry out another clear task: I cannot know in advance what I will have to reconsider in myself, or whether I will like the change.

Working together can also begin with a clear goal. We want to make something, learning from each other and helping each other through a difficult part. It matters to me that along the way we can still change the original wish itself. Another person may notice something worth leaving the earlier plan for, and I may agree before knowing what we will receive in its place. What happens between us then changes the terms by which we meant to judge success.

In this change I feel a style of my own. It exists as I change, as a familiar way of acting no longer fits what is happening and I have to look for another. With another person beside me, it is especially hard to divide this beforehand into mine and theirs. I can keep my own voice and still hear in it something I could not say before.

Something may remain from the shared work that can readily be passed on. Others may recognize a manner in it, find a good solution, repeat a particular technique. The change itself cannot be placed beside it as another finished object. It happens while the person who could present it is also changing. I try to stay attentive to it as it happens, and each time I risk deciding too soon that I already know what it was.

While everything continues, an earlier description of myself may cease to fit. What I used to explain my choice turns out to be only part of the path. I want to bear this for a while, to let another person meet me without my having to fit a familiar role at once. And to leave them the same space, even if their next step proves inconvenient for our shared plan.

There is no safe place already prepared for this wish. We can value relationships and still face hardship, exhaustion, fear of losing work. We can care for each other and find ourselves inside demands that are hard to revise. I know no way to carry everything dear beyond these conditions. It matters to me at least to notice the moment when they begin deciding what I ought to want.

In the imagined working day, the work can still end earlier. I still want to take that opportunity. But now it is harder to assign the freed time immediately to the next task, as though its fate had been known before it appeared. Among the useful continuations, I want to discern one I am actually ready to turn toward, even though I cannot yet explain its value by someone else's measure.

I leave this hour unfilled a little longer. Another person may still appear within it, with an intention of their own that changes mine. Some of what was planned will then remain undone, and I will have to live with that too. Yet the direction I take may still emerge between us, as I change.
